# Playtest 1 Nov 2026 (twelfth match) - AI v0.12.1, two AIs (Maverick, Clowrus, level 1) vs Pavol: human won at turn 35

Started from the **T14 autosave of the v0.10.1 match** (same map, same AIs, same position), so the comparison with
v0.10.1 (human won T39) is as controlled as a human match gets. Data: `docs/playtests/data/v0121-*.jsonl` (git-ignored).
No live monitor this match.

## What worked
- **Targeting is consistent.** 12 of 12 strike decisions committed to the human (v0.11.1: flip-flop every turn;
  v0.10.1: first six strikes AI vs AI). 11 executed, 1 aborted (v0.11.1: 13 of 26 aborted).
- Mine selling works: Clowrus sold 2-10 dry mines a turn from T28. Weapon cap held at 30. Max-hold fired at T30.
- Mod loaded clean: 0.12.1, 15 of 15 hooks, AbortBelowShare migrated 0.3 -> 0, no mod errors.

## Why it lost faster than v0.10.1 from the same position
1. **The energy cap choked the economy.** Saturation engaged from T20 (Clowrus 10.3k energy). Peak energy 24.0k
   (Clowrus T25) and 16.4k (Maverick T23), against 52.8-67.1k at T23-T30 in v0.10.1 from the same save. With plants
   filtered, the economy put its energy into extraction instead (Clowrus queued 14-30 Mines and their yield upgrades
   a turn, T21-T26), so the planet still ran dry at T28 while the material stock grew unused: 38.6k (T22) -> 96.2k (T28),
   material glut every turn from T22. Energy, not material, was the binding constraint: the rule was backwards in this
   state. Material is the stock and energy the throughput needed to spend it.
2. **Strikes produced little observable suppression.** The human's net building count rose (141 -> 161) and population
   grew ~100-110 a turn. Confirmed mechanisms: interception (~41% of our impacts got through, e.g. Clowrus T30 window:
   41 impacts, 24 intercepted) and dispersed, often partial damage (Railgun 125 base, x2 with the Building Damage
   upgrades the AIs bought, vs 250-400 HP; unguided hits land at a random surface rotation). **Human repair is
   unverified** (Sol v0.13: several attacked turns show no material spent; telemetry has no per-building HP).
   Separately, **the native AI never repairs** (`AIActions.RepairBuilding` is an empty stub with no caller), so damage
   on the AIs accumulates.
   Confound (Sol v0.13): the human fired first at T24 here (30 Railguns) vs T28 in v0.10.1 (none at T24), so the
   four-turn earlier defeat is not attributable to the energy cap alone.
3. **The AIs never saw the human's defenses.** `defensesBy jogi: 0` all match while the human had 19-26 Kinetic Defense.
   Strike estimates relied on the observed-interception correction alone.
4. **Weapon choice was aimed at the wrong target.** The director's weapon target was the other AI (T20-T24) while the
   strategy committed to the human; all 30 weapons per AI were Railguns into a human whose only defense was Kinetic
   (0.8 vs Railguns, 0 vs Missiles; the human had no Ion Defense). Observed Railgun pass 0.41-0.55 did not move the pick.
5. The human: 30 Railguns from T23, 14 Lasers from T29, 26 Kinetic, ~110k material banked by T35; the AIs built no
   Shields against the Lasers this time either (Ion 3 at T34-35 only).

## Next (v0.13)
1. Replace energy saturation: stop power only when energy actually went unspent (energy glut), and stop extraction
   (mines, material yield upgrades and projects) when material is in glut. Energy is the throughput; never cap it while
   material piles up.
2. **AI repair**: repair damaged buildings (cost 100 energy + 100-225 material each) with banked material, weapons
   and defenses first.
3. Weapon choice against the committed strike target, with observed pass: Missiles against a Kinetic-only defender.
4. Concentrate fire to destroy buildings (two-three hits per building) rather than spreading 6.0-radius Railgun hits.
5. Infer hidden defenses from observed interception; more intel when the target's defenses are unseen but intercepting.
