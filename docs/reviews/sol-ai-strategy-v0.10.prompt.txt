Evidence I measured from the v0.9.1 data (verify, challenge, extend):
1. Energy starvation from war. War starts T15. From T18 Guts had about 2.0-2.7k spendable energy per turn ("energyStart" in director records) out of 6.0-6.8k income; the rest went to weapon firing upkeep (~2.0-2.7k) and a defense reserve (DefenseReserveShare 0.3). It ended every turn with energy ~0, but with 15k-37k material unspent and 30-60 build slots free. It fired 35-43 Railguns every turn, mostly into enemy Kinetic Defenses.
2. The human fired nothing until T28: 16-19 Kinetic Defenses, all energy into growth, then 51 Railguns at once (T28) plus a Solar Laser. Human energy: T22 6.1k, T28 9.7k, T30 15.0k, T32 18.6k, T35 24.1k, with only 9-15 Solar Plants and 25 Floating Plants (the AIs had 23 Solar + 9 Floating at T30 and 6.8k). Several human jumps came with no new plants (multipliers / projects).
3. Research. Human order: QuantumComputing T8, ExperimentalMatterControl T11, then 4thGenNuclearReactors and LosslessEnergyTransfer by T14-15 (Lossless unlocks pro_EnergyYieldLongGlobal1, x1.3 global energy for 6 turns, 150 E + 300 M), science 1,239/turn at T12 vs the AI's 513 (science short projects). The AI's plan has 4thGen/Lossless at #30-31 and never reached them by T34; it researched weapons/defenses (EmDrives, Thermonuclear, Induction Coils, Zero Reluctance...) instead.
4. Three-player dynamics. The two AIs targeted each other heavily (Guts assigned 5 of 7 weapons to Knight at T16 because Knight's economy read higher than the human's). Knight was crippled by ~T21 and collapsed at T29; Guts collapsed in one turn at T33 (151 -> 76 buildings, 30 Kinetic Defenses did not hold against 51 Railguns at once). Pavol considers AI-vs-AI fighting legitimate (three independent players), but it may be strategically bad for each AI.
5. Interception: a defense in range intercepts ~90%; each roll costs the defender energy; defenses fail when energy runs out. Saturation (many shots in one turn) beats defenses.
